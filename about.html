About

I'm Ashwin, a master's student in astrophysics at the University of Potsdam. I work in computational astrophysics, using hydrodynamical simulations to understand how gas behaves in and around galaxies.

For my master's thesis at the Leibniz Institute for Astrophysics Potsdam (AIP), supervised by Rainer Weinberger, I study how jets from supermassive black holes carve cavities into the hot gas of galaxy groups and clusters. I run these simulations with the moving-mesh code AREPO.

From January to May 2025, I was a research intern at the Max Planck Institute for Astrophysics in Garching, working with Max Gronke and Hitesh Kishore Das. I simulated cold gas clouds in a hot, turbulent medium to find out when they survive, and the results became my first paper.

Alongside my studies, I work as a student research assistant at GFZ in Potsdam, where I write Python pipelines for auroral imagery and magnetometer data and help keep instruments at Neumayer Station III in Antarctica running from afar.

Away from the screen, I like being outdoors in nature and cooking, and I can spend far too long choosing the colours for a plot.

Other experience

Before moving to Germany, I did my BSc in Physics at Madras Christian College in Tamil Nadu, India, graduating with first-class honours in 2022.

During my master's I have also worked outside astrophysics. At Helmholtz-Zentrum Berlin, I ran GPU-accelerated micromagnetic simulations of spin waves and skyrmions with Dr. Steffen Wittrock, and built a GUI for monitoring low-frequency radio signals in superconducting cavities. I also joined a beamtime at the FERMI free-electron laser in Trieste, helping with data collection, analysis and running the experiment.

I was on the local organising committee for Thinkshop 2025 at AIP, a week-long international conference on feedback in galaxy formation. I've taken part in the WE-Heraeus and NARIT Cosmology School in Chiang Mai, Thailand, and the Jürgen Ehlers Spring School at the Max Planck Institute for Gravitational Physics. The final months of my master's are supported by a DAAD degree completion scholarship.

Day to day, I work in Python and C/C++ on HPC clusters, with AREPO and Athena++ as my main simulation codes.
