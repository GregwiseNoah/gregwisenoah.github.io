About

Research summary

My foundation is in physics: a Bachelor's from Madras Christian College (First Class Honours), covering mechanics, electromagnetism, general relativity, and quantum mechanics. I'm now finishing an MSc in Astrophysics at the University of Potsdam (expected 11/2026), where courses like Galaxies and Cosmology and Advanced Computational Astrophysics gave me a hands-on foundation in exactly the kind of multi-scale, simulation-driven astrophysics I work on today.

My research centers on how AGN jets and turbulent gas evolve. During my internship at the Max Planck Institute for Astrophysics, I used Athena++ to quantify cold-cloud survival in a turbulent medium, finding that dense clouds survive less easily in the ICM than in the CGM — work that became my first paper, published in RNAAS. My master's thesis, Carving Cavities in the Cosmos, uses Arepo to simulate AGN jets in galaxy groups and clusters, motivated by a tension where recent X-ray observations (including XRISM) show less hot gas than models predict. Comparing single-outburst and self-regulated jet simulations, I've found that low-power, long-duration jets do more damage to a halo than short, high-power bursts.

Alongside this, I've worked on micromagnetic simulations of spin-wave propagation and skyrmions at Helmholtz-Zentrum Berlin, and Python pipelines for auroral cameras and magnetometers at Neumayer Station III, Antarctica, with GFZ Potsdam. I'm comfortable across Python, C/C++, and HPC job scheduling with SLURM — and lately I've been building a small open-source Python library for colour-blind-accessible plot palettes, a side project I enjoy alongside the research.
