Research

My research interest primarily lies in computational astrophysics. My work uses hydrodynamical simulations to understand the evolution of gas in and around galaxies.

The hot gas that fills galaxy clusters are so dense that they should lose their energy by radiative cooling, leading to cool cores with high star formation rates. But observations show that these cores don't cool as much as they should. One of the leading explanations is that jets launched from supermassive black holes stir and heat up this gas, pumping energy back into these systems.

For my Master's thesis, I simulate AGN jets in galaxy groups to see how these jets affect these smaller objects.

I also did an internship which focussed on the small scale of the same gas in galaxy clusters, where the main question was to quantify how thermal instabilities can cause the growth of cold gas clumps and the conditions in which they can grow.

Carving Cavities in the Cosmos

Master's thesis, AIP, 2025–2026

How much can a black hole's jets reshape a galaxy group? I simulate resolved AGN jets with AREPO and show how these jets can affect the halo.

When do cold clouds survive?

Research internship, MPA Garching, 2025, published in RNAAS

Cold clouds in the ICM are shredded by turbulence unless they cool fast enough. Across 33 Athena++ simulations, we found they need to cool about ten times faster than the established criterion predicts.
