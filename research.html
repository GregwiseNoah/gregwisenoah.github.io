Research

My research interest primarily lies in computational astrophysics. My work involves using hydrodynamical simulations to study how gas such as the intracluster medium (ICM) behaves in large systems like galaxy clusters.

The hot ICM that fills galaxy clusters radiates the heat out and cools. Near the center, the ICM is so dense that it has very short cooling times, which should lead to cooling flows and high star formation rates. But observations show that these cores don't cool as much as they should. One of the leading explanations is that jets launched from supermassive black holes stir and heat up this gas, pumping energy back into the ambient medium.

For my master's thesis, I simulate AGN jets in galaxy groups to see how these jets affect their host systems.

At much smaller scales, my internship looked at cold gas clouds in the turbulent ICM: under what conditions they survive and grow, instead of being mixed into the hot gas.

How much can a black hole's jets reshape a galaxy group? I simulate resolved AGN jets with AREPO and show how the jets can affect the halo.

When do cold clouds survive?

Research internship, MPA Garching, 2025, published in RNAAS

Cold clouds in the ICM are shredded by turbulence unless they cool fast enough. Across 33 Athena++ simulations, we found they need to cool about ten times faster than the established criterion predicts.
